Meet Alisen#

Alisen is the intelligence layer built into the Heuristix platform. She is always present — in the canvas, in the results, and now in this textbook.

What she does. Alisen reads your workflow and explains it back to you: what each node is doing, why the results look the way they do, what could go wrong, and what to try next. She does not just describe — she interprets. When your model’s AUC drops, she will tell you the most likely reason. When you add a node she has not seen you use before, she will explain the theory behind it in plain English. When your pipeline scores 54 out of 100, she will show you exactly which nodes brought it down and what a 70+ pipeline looks like.

How she works. Alisen — Heuristix’s Artificial Learning and Intelligence Synthesis ENgine — is Heuristix’s bespoke AI that acts as your trusted second-in-command. She’s always at your side, advising, helping you ask better questions and make better decisions. She has been given deep context about data science, about the Heuristix node taxonomy, and about what good and bad pipelines look like across industries. She knows the textbook. She knows the canvas. She knows the benchmarks.

Where to find her. On the canvas, look for the chat icon in the top toolbar — click it to ask Alisen anything about your workflow, your results, or the nodes you are using. She also surfaces automatically: after every workflow run, she generates an interpretation of your results in the results panel. When you open the Intelligence panel (◎), her assessments inform the pipeline scores, audit findings, and next-step recommendations.

A word of caution. Alisen is a reasoning assistant, not an oracle. She works from the information visible in your workflow — she cannot see your raw data. When she says “this suggests class imbalance”, she means your pipeline structure makes imbalance likely; she is not reading your data directly. Treat her interpretations as hypotheses to test, not verdicts to accept.

Using Alisen with this textbook. Throughout these chapters you will see references to Heuristix nodes and concepts. If something is unclear, open Alisen on the canvas and ask. She can explain any section of any chapter in the context of your specific workflow. The textbook gives the theory; Alisen helps you apply it to your problem.

Who This Book Is For#

This textbook serves three audiences simultaneously:

Corporate Platform Users You are a business analyst, data engineer, or data scientist using Heuristix to solve real business problems. Every node you drag onto the canvas has a chapter here that explains exactly what it does, how to configure it, what the outputs mean, and when to use it instead of something else.

Data Scientists in Training You are learning the craft of data science — the real thing, not toy examples. This book does not simplify. Every chapter covers the full mathematical theory, the assumptions, the derivations, and the practical Python implementation. You will leave each chapter understanding not just how to use a technique but why it works and when it breaks.

Heuristix Academy Students You are enrolled in the Heuristix Academy, learning data science on live business problems using a production-grade platform. The same tool your corporate clients use is your classroom. Every workflow you build is a real analysis. The textbook is your companion — linking theory to practice in every session.

The Heuristix Workflow Model#

Heuristix organises all of data science into eight stages — a natural sequence from raw data to business action.

Part I
Connect
Get data in and out — files, databases, APIs, streams
Part II
Explore
Shape, clean, transform, and engineer features
Part III
Understand
Profile, test hypotheses, find patterns, validate
Part IV
Predict
Machine learning, forecasting, scoring, recommendation
Part V
Decide
Decision rules, optimisation, simulation, experimentation
Part VI
Explain
Causal inference, attribution, interpretation
Part VII
Augment
LLMs, embeddings, NLP, generative AI
Part VIII
Act
Schedule, alert, export, report

Every node on the Heuristix canvas belongs to one of these eight stages. Each part of this textbook maps directly to a stage — and every chapter maps directly to a node.

A Note on Mathematical Notation#

This book uses standard statistical and mathematical notation throughout:

  • Scalars: \(x\), \(y\), \(\alpha\), \(\beta\)

  • Vectors: \(\mathbf{x}\), \(\boldsymbol{\beta}\)

  • Matrices: \(\mathbf{X}\), \(\mathbf{W}\)

  • Estimated quantities: \(\hat{y}\), \(\hat{\beta}\)

  • Expected value: \(\mathbb{E}[X]\)

  • Probability: \(P(A)\), \(p(x)\)

  • Normal distribution: \(\mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2)\)

  • Proportional to: \(\propto\)

  • Argmin/argmax: \(\arg\min_\theta\), \(\arg\max_\theta\)

This textbook is a living document. As Heuristix adds new nodes and capabilities, new chapters are added. If you find an error or want to suggest an improvement, contact the Heuristix team.
